Module 4 Reflection- Machine Learning

Reflection

At the beginning of this Machine Learning module, I was interested in understanding how machine learning could be applied to problems I experience, particularly in healthcare. I initially found some of the concepts challenging and felt overwhelmed by the workload. Balancing my professional responsibilities and health difficulties with university study also affected my motivation and ability to fully engage. Reflecting on my experience has helped me recognise both the knowledge I have developed and the areas I need to improve and I have considered what I experienced, what I learned from it and how I can apply this learning in the future (Rolfe et al., 2001).

One of the most valuable aspects of the module was developing an understanding of different machine learning approaches and their applications. Through the Airbnb team project, I explored the potential of regression and clustering to address a business problem. The project investigated how listing prices could be predicted using property features and how neighbourhoods could be grouped into market segments. My individual contribution focused on the business implications of these approaches, considering how predictive pricing and segmentation could support revenue forecasting, host profitability and more targeted pricing strategies. This helped me appreciate that the value of data science extends beyond developing a model; analytical outputs also need to be interpreted and translated into meaningful decisions. Clustering, for example, can reveal patterns and groupings within data that may not be immediately apparent (Jain, 2010).

I also developed my understanding of similarity measures through a Jaccard coefficient exercise, comparing patient records using their shared positive features. Initially, I found it difficult to distinguish between the intersection and union, particularly when identifying which features should be included in the calculation. By working through the comparisons, I developed an understanding of how the coefficient measures similarity and this exercise demonstrated the importance of understanding how data are represented and coded before applying a mathematical method. Although it was a relatively small activity, it reinforced the value of breaking unfamiliar problems into manageable steps rather than becoming discouraged when I did not immediately understand them.

My understanding of neural networks developed further through the MNIST reassessment, which involved building and evaluating an artificial neural network to classify handwritten digits. This was a particularly valuable learning experience because I had to engage with the practical stages of a machine learning workflow, including preparing and normalising data, creating training and validation sets, training the model and evaluating its performance. I initially found some of the terminology and processes difficult to understand, but working through the notebook in stages helped me develop greater confidence. The model achieved approximately 97.86% accuracy on the test dataset, although 214 out of 10,000 images were misclassified. Reviewing the confusion matrix and individual errors helped me understand why accuracy alone does not provide a complete evaluation of model performance as well as where and why a model makes incorrect predictions. This practical work gave me an opportunity to connect theoretical concepts with the implementation and evaluation of a neural network (Goodfellow et al., 2016).

There was a significant change to my assessment experience between the initial team proposal in Unit 6 and the final assessment period. Due to extenuating circumstances and illness, I was unable to complete the planned Unit 11 team project and instead undertook the individual MNIST reassessment, this unfortunately means I could compare the completed team project against the original proposal. I will, however, touch on how the change affected my learning. The individual reassessment gave me the opportunity to focus on the technical implementation of a machine learning model and develop practical skills independently. I missed the opportunity to experience the full collaborative development process and contribute to the final team project. This highlighted that technical learning and collaborative learning are both important, but provide different experiences and opportunities for development.

My experience of working within my team was very insightful, where we communicated through WhatsApp which allowed us to share updates, discuss progress and remain connected throughout the project. I engaged with my teammates and learned from observing their contributions and approaches to the work. I would say that balancing work commitments with university deadlines sometimes affected my ability to contribute as consistently as I would have liked. I communicated when I experienced delays, and it appeared that other team members were also managing competing commitments, which was reassuring. In the future I would like to take on a greater leadership role, particularly in coordinating contributions and bringing the final presentation together. I was quite impressed with the way my team member Naeema coordinated the project and brought everything together. Although I contributed to the business analysis, I recognise that taking more responsibility for organisation and communication would have helped towards future career opportunities that may arise.

The module also encouraged me to consider the wider professional implications of machine learning and emerging technologies, where discussions around Industry 4.0 and Industry 5.0 helped me consider the growing role of automation and artificial intelligence in healthcare, alongside the importance of human involvement, resilience and cybersecurity. The Synnovis cyber attack demonstrated how disruption to digital infrastructure can affect pathology services and patient care. Exploring AI writing tools encouraged me to consider issues of accuracy, bias and the need for human oversight. These discussions were particularly relevant to my profession as a Biomedical Scientist, where data accuracy, confidentiality and patient safety are essential. I have developed a greater appreciation that technological advancement must be accompanied by appropriate professional judgement and responsibility.

I feel more confident in my understanding of machine learning than I did at the beginning of the module. Completing the MNIST reassessment was particularly important in rebuilding my confidence because I was able to dedicate time to understanding the work rather than simply trying to complete tasks. I recognise that my engagement could have been more consistent throughout the module, however this is due to living with a chronic health condition, and I recognise that I should reach out for help and not feel embarrassed or inadequate because of it. Going forward, I intend to approach complex tasks incrementally, plan my study time more consistently and become more proactive in collaborative activities. I would also like to develop my technical skills further and explore how data science can complement my existing experience in the lab. I will look to see how I can implement what I learned towards Laboratory Information Management Systems (LIMS) to further understand my role and what I can contribute to it.

References

  • Goodfellow, I., Bengio, Y. and Courville, A. (2016) Deep Learning. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press
  • Jain, A.K. (2010) ‘Data clustering: 50 years beyond K-means’, Pattern Recognition Letters, 31(8), pp. 651–666
  • Rolfe, G., Freshwater, D. and Jasper, M. (2001) Critical Reflection for Nursing and the Helping Professions: A User’s Guide. Basingstoke: Palgrave Macmillan.
