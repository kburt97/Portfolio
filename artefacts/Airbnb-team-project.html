Artefact: Air BnB Team Project

Machine Learning Team Project .

Overview

This artefact documents my contribution to a collaborative Airbnb business analysis project (Classical Machine Learning – Regression and Clustering). The project used the 2019 New York City Airbnb dataset to investigate how regression models and clustering techniques could support listing price prediction and neighbourhood market segmentation. My individual contribution focused on evaluating the business implications of these approaches, particularly their potential to improve pricing strategies, revenue forecasting, host profitability and targeted business decisions.

My Contribution- Business Implications

The price prediction and neighbourhood segmentation outputs generate several actionable insights for Airbnb’s strategic decision making around pricing and host support in the NYC market. Predictive pricing enhances revenue forecasting accuracy by estimating expected listing values based on observable property and location attributes.

Accurate forecasts help align host pricing behaviour with actual demand, reducing revenue leakage from underpricing and improving host profitability (Zervas et al., 2017). This also enables Airbnb to refine commission forecasts, supporting strategic financial planning.

Market segmentation uncovers customer and listing clusters, allowing Airbnb to tailor pricing strategies and platform support services. For example, segmentation can differentiate premium urban core listings from mid range or budget listings in outer boroughs, enabling targeted pricing recommendations and service tiering. Research shows that segmenting by location and amenities reveals commercially distinct market groups with different pricing and demand characteristics (Guttentag, 2013). By tailoring pricing guidance, visibility tools and marketing incentives to segment specific characteristics, Airbnb can allocate promotional spend more efficiently, maximising returns across neighbourhood markets.

Combining regression based price prediction with clustering enhances operational decisions by enabling neighbourhood level pricing guidance and differentiated host support, while maintaining model interpretability, a requirement for platform governance and host adoption (Einav et al., 2016). Interpretable analytics allow Airbnb to justify pricing to hosts, reinforcing trust and compliance. This classical ML approach translates into actionable strategic levers including: targeted pricing, segment specific incentives, and more predictable revenue outcomes, strengthening competitive positioning in one of Airbnb’s most data rich markets.

Reflection

Contributing to the Airbnb Classical Machine Learning project helped me develop a better understanding of how machine learning outputs can be translated into meaningful business decisions. Focusing on the business implications allowed me to look beyond the technical application of regression and clustering and consider how these methods could support pricing strategies, revenue forecasting and market segmentation.

It was valuable to consider how combining price prediction with neighbourhood segmentation could provide more targeted insights for Airbnb and its hosts. This highlighted the importance of interpreting analytical results in the context of the problem they are intended to address, rather than viewing model performance in isolation.

Working as part of a team also helped me appreciate how different areas of analysis contribute to a more comprehensive solution. It strengthened my ability to communicate the practical value of data science findings and consider their wider commercial implications. Overall, this project developed my understanding of how classical machine learning techniques can support evidence based strategic decision making.
