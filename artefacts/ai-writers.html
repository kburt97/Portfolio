Artefact: AI writers Discussion

  • Critical evaluation of Robo-writers and the use of AI writing and
  • thoughts on the pitfalls as well as the benefits of using AI writing
  • tools at different levels particularly its use within laboratory
  • science and healthcare.

    • Overview

    This artefact explores the use of AI writing tools and their growing influence on academic, professional and creative writing. It considers their potential benefits, including improving efficiency and supporting communication, alongside limitations such as accuracy, bias, originality and the importance of human judgement.

    Initial Post

    AI writing tools are increasingly being used across administrative, academic and creative settings. Hutson (2021) discusses that language generating AI can produce convincing text, offering opportunities to improve efficiency but also raising concerns about accuracy and originality.

    In administrative settings, AI writers can assist with drafting emails, reports and summarising information, reducing time spent on routine tasks. In healthcare laboratories, for example, they could help prepare non-confidential documentation and improve the clarity of written communication. In academic and creative writing, these tools can support brainstorming, proofreading and generating ideas.

    However, their limitations should not be overlooked, as AI generated content may contain inaccurate information, reflect biases within training data or produce convincing statements without genuinely understanding their meaning (Bender et al., 2021). This is particularly concerning in healthcare, where inaccurate information could have serious consequences. Research also highlights the importance of checking AI generated scientific content and references rather than accepting them uncritically (Kollman et al., 2023).

    AI writers should be viewed as supportive tools rather than replacements for human judgement, creativity and responsibility.

    Summary Post

    AI writing tools have the potential to transform how written information is produced, from routine administrative tasks to academic and creative writing.  Having considered Hutson's (2021) discussion of language generating AI, I believe these technologies offer considerable benefits, provided their limitations are recognised and appropriate human oversight is maintained.

    >In administrative environments, AI writers can improve efficiency by drafting emails, summarising meetings, preparing reports and organising information.  In laboratory healthcare, this could support the preparation of standard operating procedures (SOPs), staff communications and non-clinical documentation, allowing professionals to dedicate more time to operational and patient focused responsibilities.  AI can also assist with academic writing, brainstorming and improving the clarity of complex information.

    However, these benefits introduce significant risks, where Hutson (2021) highlights that language generating AI can produce convincing text without genuinely understanding its meaning.  This creates the possibility of inaccurate information being presented confidently.   Bender et al. (2021) similarly discuss how language models can reproduce biases and generate misleading content based on their training data.   In healthcare, these limitations are particularly concerning because inaccurate documentation or misleading clinical information could potentially compromise patient safety.  Harrer (2023) emphasises the importance of human oversight when applying large language models in healthcare.

    Creative writing also raises questions surrounding originality, intellectual property and the potential displacement of human creativity.   Although AI can generate ideas and assist with written expression, it may lack the personal experiences and emotional understanding that contribute to genuinely original work (Koplin, 2023).

    My peer Mansour also raises a significant point regarding bias and the potential risk associated with machine learning systems exhibiting bias due to training models used.  This is an important consideration to make, particularly when discussing the use of AI in healthcare, due to the potential negative effects that could impact patient care.  Training models may represent a particular race, gender or sexuality in a skewed fashion, and this could pose a risk when implementing to real world practices.  It is important to factor in, and mitigate with human intervention and continuous testing as Mansour has mentioned. 

    Overall, I believe AI writers should be viewed as supportive tools rather than replacements for human judgement.  Their outputs should be critically evaluated, fact checked and reviewed before being used, particularly in healthcare environments.  Responsible implementation requires transparency, data protection and clear accountability.  Ultimately, the greatest benefit of AI writing technology will come from combining its efficiency with human expertise, critical thinking and professional responsibility.

    Reflection

    This discussion allowed me to explore the growing role of artificial intelligence in generating written content and critically consider its applications across different industries. Reading Hutson (2021) helped me understand how language generating AI systems work and the opportunities they present, particularly in improving efficiency and reducing the time spent on routine administrative tasks.

    I found it particularly interesting to consider how these technologies could be applied within laboratory healthcare. For example, AI writing tools could assist with drafting communications, summarising information and preparing administrative documentation. However, I also recognised that their use in healthcare requires particular caution because inaccurate or misleading information could have implications for clinical practice and patient safety.

    Another important area of learning was understanding the limitations of AI generated content, including hallucinations, bias and the potential impact on originality and creativity. This encouraged me to think more critically about the information produced by these systems rather than automatically accepting it as accurate.

    Completing this discussion also made me reflect how I could use AI within my role as a Biomedical Scientist, working within Haematology and Blood Transfusion. Consideration towards its usage in the context of SOP writing and document control has given me scope for exploration, and has allowed me to consider how I can be more efficient in the administrative side of my role. These contributions have been widely received my senior management, and implementation looks promising.

    Overall, this activity improved my awareness of the opportunities and limitations of generative AI. It reinforced the importance of human judgement, critical thinking and responsible AI use when applying machine learning technologies to real world situations.
